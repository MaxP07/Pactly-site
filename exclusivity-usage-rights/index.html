Exclusivity, usage rights and boosting

A publication date, an exclusivity period and permission to promote content can belong to the same brand deal. Keeping each clear helps you organise commitments and assess the next opportunity. Start with the terms actually agreed in your contract or brief.

Exclusivity: record the scope and dates

When a brief contains an exclusivity or non-compete term, record the categories and competitor brands explicitly named, together with the restriction dates. Ask for clarification if the scope is unclear.

Paclara compares opportunities against the categories, competitor brands and restriction dates you enter. It does not infer competitors or invent missing terms.

Usage rights: check how the content may be used

Look for the content covered, permitted channels, territory and duration wherever these are specified in your agreement. Keep the agreed wording with the collaboration for reference.

Paclara can distinguish creator-posted content, content with brand usage rights and brand-only UGC. This helps describe the work when organising a deal and considering its price.

Boosting: keep paid promotion on its own timeline

Record the agreed paid-media terms and dates separately from exclusivity. Do not assume that permission to boost content creates a restriction on another brand collaboration, or that an exclusivity period grants usage rights.

An example with three end points

Suppose content publishes on 14 June, an explicitly agreed category restriction ends on 20 June, and boosting ends on 28 June. A proposed deal on 23 June falls after that restriction but during boosting. Dates alone do not establish a contractual conflict: review the entered categories, competitor brands and actual agreed terms.

Use potential overlaps as a prompt to review

Paclara uses deterministic checks to surface potential overlaps based on your entries. A flag prompts you to check the relevant agreements. It is not a legal conclusion about a breach, enforceability or permission to accept a deal. Consult a qualified professional for contractual uncertainty.

Bring terms into your workflow

Keep the brief and commitments in your brand deal tracker. When preparing a quote, record the new scope and use completed history as context for creator pricing.
